Lightning guideline · Coders

Part D is Coders: the For Geeks section of lightningeg.com, the pages for developers who read, run and build Lightning. The rest of the site is Normal, Part B. Coders has its own look: a terminal on a dark field, monospace type and its themes. This document is what that look is made of, so a new Coders page matches the three that exist, and so a developer finds what to do and how to do it in one tap.

A · AppB · NormalC · PhoneD · Coders

Four documents, one folder. This is the Coders document (the For Geeks pages). For the app on a PC open app.html, for the Normal website website.html, for the phone phone.html. Coders shares the logo, the facts and the voice (brand-voice.md) with the others; its colours, type and blocks are its own and never cross into Normal pages.

D01Pages

Coders is a section of its own, with its own top bar. Each page answers one developer's question, and the first screen after the hero answers it.

homeWhere can I help? Where we need you and the three steps, then why the code is open and the proof · forgeeks.html
→ Pick a task
contributeHow do I send a change? Pick it, change it, send it, every click in plain words, then the rules, the team and the reference · forgeeks-guide.html
→ Pick a task
roadmapWhat comes next? M1 core and workflow with the plan codes, M2 Google Play, M3 App Store · forgeeks-roadmap.html
→ Pick a task
  • Three open pages, six member pages. Member pages need no hero: account (forgeeks-account.html: sign in, profile, GitHub, activity, your data), tasks, market, leaderboard, profile (?u=) and admin (owner only). They read the signed-in member through js/forgeeks-auth.js (window.FG, one Supabase client) and show nothing private without a session. for-geeks.html redirects to forgeeks.html; forgeeks-collaborate.html redirects to contribute with its old sections mapped. for-geeks-2.html, for-geeks-4.html and for-geeks-test.html keep earlier concepts: never copy from them.
  • One path, said once. The three steps are written in full on contribute only. Home shows them in three cells and links there; every other page links. Any fact lives on one page, and the others link to it.
  • No dead ends. Every page closes with its next step: a primary button to the open tasks or to contribute.
  • A new Coders page loads css/forgeeks.css and js/forgeeks.js and nothing from Normal (no landing.css, footer.py footer, signup band or testing panel). Its own styles go in forgeeks.css under a commented heading; a member page also loads css/forgeeks-account.css (shared forms) and may have one css/forgeeks-<page>.css. Its top bar, footer and scripts come from tools/geeks_shell.py: add it to PAGES (in the top bar) or EXTRA (member pages) and run it; tools/site_check.py fails when a page drifts.
  • Every open Coders page and the leaderboard is indexed, has a canonical URL and social tags, and is listed in sitemap.xml and llms.txt; the other member pages are noindex and left out. Normal pages link to Coders from the footer (More).
  • The roadmap stays in step with the app repository's NOW.md and issues/INDEX.md; its items carry their plan codes (7B, 6B).

D03.1Mono is free; other palettes are owned cosmetics

Owner policy revision: Mono is the only free website palette. Other palettes require account ownership. Existing purchases remain owned; previously equipped free palettes are preserved during migration. Prices live in the server catalog. Main-site light/dark accessibility controls remain available.

  • Contrast: in every theme, ink and dim measure 4.5:1 or more on bg, panel and well, and btn-ink on btn. A new theme passes that before it ships; python tools/theme_contrast.py checks every theme.
  • Five on sale (owner, 2026-10-25): Paper, Frost, Meadow, Nile and Garnet, each working on both sites before any more are added. Archived: Ocean, Tokyo, Dracula, Rose, Synthwave, Phosphor, Amber, Solarized and Moss are no longer sold or shown, but a member who owns one keeps it, sees it and can wear it; their tokens stay in css/forgeeks.css. How a theme is made, part by part, for both sites: themes.md.
  • Paid themes are bought in the market with Lightning Coins. Anyone may preview them in the picker; keeping one needs it owned, and the picker shows a lock and the price. Paper and Frost are the light themes. Accessibility is never paid for: contrast, motion and keyboard rules hold in every theme.
  • Mono is the default and the fallback for an unknown saved theme. Paper keeps color-scheme: light dark so a phone that darkens websites leaves it light. Meadow uses Lightning's Nile night, green and azure.
  • A theme changes colours only, never sizes, spacing or words. The browser's theme-color follows the theme's bg.
  • Not in Coders: the vivid gradient, Green/Azure/Rose money meanings, raw hex values in a page's own styles (use a token), a colour that differs between pages.

A wide, quiet column over the field. One idea per section.

  • Cells in a grid are joined by 1px hairlines on --line, drawn by each cell (a shadow to its right and below) inside the grid's border, never by a grid background showing through. A box never sits inside another box.
  • Cells over the field (cards, steps, stats, the stack's rows) use --soft: bg at 94% (--veil) in the middle, fading to 30% (--veil-edge) at the edges, so a box melts into the field instead of sitting on it like a slab. The terminal and code blocks stay a solid well at 94%, for reading. Text keeps a soft bg halo so a dot never cuts a letter.
  • Grid children, lanes included, set min-width:0: a long code line scrolls inside its box, never the page.

D08The field and motion

The page sits on a field of light. Motion is the weather, never the content.

  • The field: one fixed canvas behind the page (Mono's is a particle terrain), a 24px pixel grid fading at the edges, and faint scanlines (15% on Paper). Full under the hero and the closing, faint between them. It is built once and never rebuilds while you scroll.
  • Mono's field: the particle terrain with lightning: a strike every 6–12 seconds, full over the hero and the closing and about half as bright between them, where people read; one at full strength where you click empty space. On a PC the hero's words flash with it. On a phone (under 760px) a strike is two strokes, with no full-screen glow and no flash, and the field draws at 1.5× pixels: a strike never stalls scrolling. A faint current runs through the pixel grid now and then, and with each event.
  • Each theme draws its own scene (owner, 2026-10-26), on the same canvas, in its --particle and --glow: Paper and Cotton, ink contour lines of a slow landscape, where a drop lands and its rings push the lines; Nile and Papyrus, a river at night: stars, a gold moon and its road of light on drifting water, where a shooting star falls and lands in a ring; Garnet, embers rising from a low glow, with a burst of sparks; Frost, snow falling at three depths, with an aurora that opens over the horizon and closes in 5 seconds; Dendera, the temple ceiling's rows of five-pointed stars, each breathing, with a pigment light walking one row; Meadow, tall grass in the wind and blinking fireflies, with far-off lightning and a gust. A theme without a scene (the archived ones) keeps Mono's terrain. Every scene keeps the terrain's rules: full under the hero and the closing, faint between, one event every 6–12 seconds or where you click, fewer pieces on a phone, no blur, a still frame under reduced motion. Trying a theme on in the picker shows its scene and plays its event once.
  • Small motion: sections rise 18px into place (0.8s) as soon as their top enters the view, however tall they are; a grid of cells rises as one. Bars fill when seen (1.4s), the caret blinks, the stack's pulse runs its whole line (CSS, nothing measured), the scroll hint nudges, the hero word decodes only while the hero is in view. On Home the hero arrives once: the greeting types, then the H1, the lede and the meta fade up 12px a beat apart (0.8s each, within 1.2s); buttons work from the first frame. Easing cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1).
  • Reduced motion: one still frame of the field, no weather, no reveal, caret steady, bars drawn full. The page must read the same.
  • Motion never moves text the reader is reading, never loops faster than once a second, and never needs to finish before a button works.

D09Navigation

The top bar holds the pages; the rail holds this page's sections. Contribute is one tap from anywhere.

  • Top bar: the white mark and lightning (home), the crumb ~/root/for-geeks with a blinking caret, then the pages in lower case: home, contribute, roadmap, and app (a new tab) from 641px up; the theme chip, source ↗ and the account chip: sign in (a plain link to the account page), or once signed in the avatar and display name, which opens a menu (my profile, tasks, market, leaderboard, profile settings, sign out; ↑ ↓ Home End move, Esc closes). The current page is Ink with a › in Accent; a 1px Accent line under the bar shows reading progress. It is written by tools/geeks_shell.py, never by hand.
  • Rail: at 1300px and wider, the sections down the left edge under // on this page; the current one is a 28px Accent line. Under 1300px, on phones and laptops, the same links are a strip under the top bar that scrolls sideways; the current section is marked and kept in view. Its words are the section labels (01 / path → path). Hidden over the hero.
  • Same tab inside Coders; a new tab for Normal pages, GitHub and anything outside (target="_blank" rel="noopener").
  • Theme picker: a window in the middle of the screen, opened by the chip or T; arrows preview, Enter keeps, Esc restores. The choice is saved as lightning-geeks-theme and applied before first paint.

D10Words

The Lightning voice, said to a developer: short, exact, checkable.

Don't trust us. Run it.A claim the reader can test, with the command next to it
Blazing-fast, military-grade security!Adjectives with nothing to run
  • brand-voice.md applies. On Coders, name the file, the test or the number: "one process, one SQLite file", not "simple architecture".
  • Say what isn't done yet, plainly, in its own section. Never promise a date the roadmap doesn't hold.
  • UI words are lower case and mono, like commands: home, roadmap, copied to clipboard. Sentences are sentence case.
  • Every command shown runs as written on a fresh clone; every terminal answer is true of the current build.
  • One wording for the path: pick it, change it, send it; /take, a Codespace, a pull request with Closes #123. Never four steps on one page and three on another.
  • Where a task code appears, say how it reads once: [3C] is item C of milestone 3 of the plan.

D11Access and phones

Dark and clever is no excuse for hard to use.

  • A skip link first; focus is a 2px Accent outline, offset 3px. Every control works with the keyboard.
  • The canvas, grid and scanlines are aria-hidden and ignore the pointer; the terminal has a real input with a label.
  • Breakpoints: 1300 (rail, or the strip under it), 1100, 900, 820, 760 (crumb and theme name go), 640 (app link and source go, sections 72px), 560, 520, 420 (the wordmark goes; the mark stays). At 360px wide nothing scrolls sideways, and the top bar still holds home, contribute, roadmap, the theme chip and the account chip.
  • On a phone the terrain is lighter and steady; touch targets are at least 34px, buttons 46px.
